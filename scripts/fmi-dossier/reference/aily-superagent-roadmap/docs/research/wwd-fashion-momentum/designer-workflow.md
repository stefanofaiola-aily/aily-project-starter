## 6. Design the Instagram and media snapshot

Add a compact **Public snapshot · 27 Oct 2026** area, separate from the score signals. Show available profile handle/followers, selected-post responses and a small number of linked media examples. Keep the full research accessible in details.

- Label responses as **selected posts**, with the actual sample count, post dates and observation date. The sample is not a complete feed, reach measure or full-account engagement rate.
- Show unavailable likes/comments as unavailable. For PacSun, three inspected own-account posts with hidden likes must not become zero engagement.
- Retain sampling exceptions: J.Crew's campaign pilot and recent supplements differ from the primary Sep 20–Oct 19 visible-grid sample. Counters were observed Oct 27, 06:21–06:26 UTC and at unequal post ages.
- Keep related-account posts separate, including Polo Ralph Lauren and creator/corporate examples. For Exemplar, show Neiman Marcus, Saks and Bergdorf Goodman separately; never sum their followers.
- Label media links with publisher, publication date and source type. Preserve older articles, affiliate coverage, owned pages and company releases as such. Where publication dates are unverified, say so. Belmont's missing verified media example needs a compact empty state.

**Output:** one snapshot component with populated, hidden-counter, multiple-account and missing-media examples. No social/media performance ranking is supported by this sample.

## 7. Design the partial brand board

Use the five available draft indices: **Coach 75, Fendi 69, Ralph Lauren 68, Burberry 64, Calvin Klein 62**. Label the view **Partial · provisional · 5 of 18 brands**. Reuse the existing bar-board pattern if available, with current-brand highlighting when that brand appears. Keep any numeral scale consistent and distinguish index values from growth percentages.

Make brand details accessible from each scored row. Brands without an index remain unranked, with an explicit unavailable state; do not give them zero-length bars or positions in the ranking. Include a tie treatment in the reusable component without inventing tied scores in the populated examples.

Use this same board in all seven retailer environments, without a retailer rank or score. Keep retailer-specific Retail Momentum as an open future method. The highest current draft does not establish a showroom winner; that selection follows comparable scoring and QA.

**Output:** a partial-board view, current-brand highlight behaviour, unranked/absent-brand behaviour, and the shared retailer variant.

## 8. Add each prospect's invitation and CTA

Use the short invitation in the delivery table and the exact button label **Unlock November signal** across every variant. Keep the invitation tied to the prospect's proposed internal-data opportunity.

Show the click's next step in the prototype, marked **proposed — destination/confirmation pending product decision** until resolved. The Partner Lounge congratulations popup is one recorded proposal. Do not claim a report is delivered, a subscription activated or data connected before a real flow exists. If a future data-request flow is agreed, take its fields from the structured prospect package.

**Output:** CTA placement and a reviewable next-step proposal, with the unresolved destination clearly recorded. Annotate the click for the app team to use its authenticated analytics convention.

## 9. Connect the review flow

Create a clickable path from **WWD event card → Fashion Momentum → evidence details / brand board → invitation CTA → proposed next step**. Reuse existing navigation and dismissal conventions. Check text contrast, signed metric labels, keyboard focus, readable status labels and accessible names for details controls. Annotate these behaviours for implementation.

**Output:** a clickable prototype covering the core score flow and each alternative data state, including return navigation.

## 10. Populate all prospects from the same components

Prepare a review sheet or populated instances for **all 25 prospects across 26 mappings** using the delivery table. Reuse Marc Jacobs content across its two environments. For retailers, vary their own snapshot and invitation while retaining the same shared brand board.

Prioritize review examples that test the edge cases: Coach, Burberry, TOM FORD, Louboutin, J.Crew, PacSun, VS, Exemplar and Belmont. These are examples to review first; the final coverage still includes every mapped environment.

**Output:** a coverage table linking each prospect/environment to its design instance, component state, data reference and unresolved gap. This is a design mapping; live environment IDs require app verification.

## 11. Review the designs against the evidence

Use the delivery table's per-row gaps for the product/data review. Check exact values, source scope, dates, negative growth, provisional labels, parent/brand mappings, hidden counters, invitations and partial-board treatment. Track decisions in the reference before changing the designs or generated content.

Designs can be reviewed with the present data. Promoting indices to production and choosing showroom content require validation of comparable current/reference measurements and proxy use. One comparison remains growth only; a current value alone cannot establish growth.

**Output:** reviewed designs with a resolved/open decision log. Distinguish design completion from data validation and release readiness.

The app handoff must cover card placement, routing for all 26 environments, shared-board reuse, real CTA destination and recorded click. Provide QA cases for the five variants, negative growth, hidden counters, missing articles, brand highlights and parent/brand mappings. The original handoff names **Jimena López-Barbado Salmerón and Franziska Schlauss** to test the environments and showroom, with **Peter/Reynaldo** for delivery.

**Output:** a reviewable implementation handoff and QA checklist. The showroom design can use the same report components, with the eligible brand/content left pending comparable scoring and QA.
